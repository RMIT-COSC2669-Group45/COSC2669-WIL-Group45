# Rent payments and rent in advance
A rental agreement (lease) must state how much the rent is and how often it must be paid.

Rent must be paid to the rental provider, who may be an agent, an owner, a site owner or an operator of a rooming house or caravan park.

Renters must pay rent according to the rental agreement.

There are rules about requesting and accepting rent in advance, providing receipts and requesting additional fee payments from renters.

Related information is available about:

- [late or unpaid rent](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/late-or-unpaid-rent)

## Methods of payment
The renter and rental provider should agree on how rent will be paid before starting the rental agreement.

Before they agree, the rental provider must tell the renter about any costs associated with the selected payment method.

The rental provider must:

- provide at least one payment method that does not require the renter to pay a fee, other than the renter’s own bank fees
- allow rent to be paid through the Commonwealth Centrepay bill-paying service and another form of electronic funds transfer
- make the renter aware of any fees that may result from using the agreed payment method, such as dishonour fees
- not require the renter to pay using a post-dated cheque

Neither the renter nor the rental provider can ask the other to reimburse the costs of:

- setting up accounts to make or receive rental payments
- expenses associated with making or receiving payments, such as bank fees

Any change to how rent is paid must be agreed upon by the renter and rental provider.

## Illegal fees and charges

### Rental provider
Rental providers cannot charge fees or fines for:

- issuing a rent payment card
- establishing or using direct debit facilities
- processing rent payments
- late payment of rent

Rental providers cannot include clauses in rental agreements requiring these fines or fees to be paid. Such clauses are illegal.

### Third-party businesses
Rental providers and agents may use a third-party platform to manage a renter’s rental agreement and rent payments.

If a renter pays rent or applies for a rental property through a third-party platform, the business operating that platform must not charge the renter a fee.

> **Note:** This restriction does not include the renter’s standard bank fees.


## How often and when to pay
The rental agreement states whether rent will be paid weekly, fortnightly, monthly or at another agreed interval.

Once the rental agreement starts, rent is due on the agreed date using the agreed payment method.

Renters paying by electronic funds transfer should allow at least 24 hours for the payment to reach the rental provider’s account. For example, the renter can transfer the rent one day before the due date to avoid the payment being considered late.


## Charging rent in advance
When a renter starts a rental agreement, they are usually required to pay rent in advance before moving into the property.

A rental provider cannot ask for or accept more than one month’s rent in advance unless:

- rent is paid weekly, in which case the rental provider can only request 14 days’ rent in advance
- rent is more than $900 per week, in which case the advance payment may be up to the full value of the rental agreement

### Rent in advance in rooming houses, caravan parks and residential parks and villages
Special rules apply to rooming houses, caravan parks, residential parks and villages with Part 4A site agreements. Read more about properties covered by [Part 4A site agreements](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/site-agreements-in-residential-parks-and-villages).

The advance-payment limits are:

- rooming house operators cannot charge more than 14 days’ rent in advance
- caravan park owners cannot charge more than 14 days’ rent in advance for site rental
- caravan owners cannot charge more than 28 days’ hiring charge in advance for caravan rental
- Part 4A site owners cannot charge more than one month’s rent in advance

## Stopping rent payments
Renters must not stop paying rent until they have agreed with the rental provider on a date to stop.

Renters must continue paying rent even if:

- the rental provider or owner refuses to complete [repairs](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/repairs/repairs-in-rental-properties)
- it is the final month of the rental agreement
- the renter has given notice to end the rental agreement and leave the property
- the renter has received a notice to vacate

## Providing rent receipts
Rental providers or their agents must provide renters with receipts for rent payments:

- immediately when rent is paid in person
- within five business days after receiving a request

Rental providers must keep rent-payment records for at least 12 months.

A rent receipt must contain:

- the rental provider’s name and signature
- the property address
- the payment date
- the period covered by the payment
- the amount paid
- a clear statement that the document is a rent receipt

Rental providers can be fined for failing to follow the rent-receipt requirements.

## Overpaid rent
Rent under a residential rental agreement generally accrues from day to day. Overpaid rent is recoverable or refundable on that basis.

## Relevant legislation
The rules concerning rent payments and rent in advance are covered by these sections of the [*Residential Tenancies Act 1997*](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/residential-tenancies-act-1997):

- Sections 39–49 – Rents for residential rental agreements
- Section 51A – Prohibited rental and application fees
- Sections 99–107 – Rent for rooming houses
- Sections 150–160 – Rent and hiring charges for caravan parks
- Sections 206S–206ZC – Rent and other charges for Part 4A sites
