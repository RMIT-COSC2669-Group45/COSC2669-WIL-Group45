# Rent increases
From 25 November 2025, the minimum notice period for rental providers to issue a rent increase notice changed from 60 days to 90 days.

Learn more about [changes to Victorian rental laws](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/new-changes-to-the-rental-laws).

## Rules for increasing rent

A rental provider can increase the rent for their property, but they must follow the applicable rental laws.

A rental provider cannot increase the rent during a fixed-term rental agreement unless the agreement contains an additional term allowing an increase. If an increase is permitted, the agreement must also state how the increase will be calculated.

To increase the rent, the rental provider must give the renter a [Notice of proposed rent increase (Word, 72 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-proposed-rent-increase-to-renter-of-rented-premises.docx).

The notice must:

- be provided using the method agreed to in the rental agreement
- be given at least 90 days before the proposed increase takes effect
- allow additional delivery time if it is sent by post.

See [Australia Post delivery time estimates](https://auspost.com.au/sending/delivery-speeds-and-coverage) when sending the notice by post.

These rules apply to:

- rented residential premises
- rooming houses
- caravan parks.

Different rules apply to [rent increases under a residential park site agreement](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/rent-increases-in-site-agreements).

### Important information

- A rent increase may be invalid if the applicable rent-increase rules are not followed.
- Renters can [challenge a rent increase](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/challenging-rent-increases-or-high-rent) if they believe it is higher than the market range.
- There are limits on how often rent can be increased.

## How much rent can increase

The law does not specify exactly how much a rental provider can increase the rent.

However, the rental provider must explain how the proposed increase was calculated.

A rental provider cannot increase rent during a fixed-term rental agreement unless the agreement permits it. If the agreement allows an increase, it must state how the increase will be calculated.

The rental provider must notify the renter using the [Notice of rent increase form](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/rent-increases#forms-you-might-need) at least 90 days before the increase is due to begin.

The notice must:

- specify the amount of the rent increase
- explain the method used to calculate the increase
- calculate the increase consistently with the method stated in the notice.

### Common calculation methods

The four most common methods for calculating a rent increase are:

- **Consumer Price Index (CPI):** Based on general price changes reported by the [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/consumer-price-index-australia).
- **Rent index:** Based on average Victorian rent prices reported through the [metropolitan or regional rent index](https://www.dffh.vic.gov.au/publications/rental-report).
- **Fixed percentage:** A specified percentage increase, such as 3%.
- **Fixed dollar amount:** A specified dollar increase, such as $10.

Rental providers may use another calculation method. However:

- Referring to a calculation method without explaining it is not sufficient.
- The calculation information in the rental agreement and notice must be clear.
- The renter must be able to determine how the increase was calculated.
- The renter must be able to determine whether the increase matches the rental agreement.

For example, a rental agreement may state that rent will increase by $5 every 12 months. The rental provider may therefore issue a notice increasing the rent by $5 after the required period.

### Examples of invalid and valid wording

These examples provide general guidance and are not an exhaustive list.

#### Invalid wording

The following wording does not explain the calculation clearly enough:

- “Rent increased by dollar amount.”
- “Based on the rental market valuation.”
- “As per rental provider’s instructions.”
- “All properties in the same building are leased between [price range].”
- “CPI.”

#### Valid wording

The following examples identify a specific increase and its calculation method:

- “The next rent increase will be by 1.8%, in line with the Consumer Price Index increase reported by the Australian Bureau of Statistics for the September 2025 quarter.”
- “The next rent increase will be by $20, in line with the statewide rent increase for the June 2025 quarter.”

If an estate agent manages the property, the agent must prepare the Notice of rent increase correctly and obtain clear instructions from the rental provider. An incorrectly prepared notice may breach the agent’s professional conduct obligations.

A renter who believes a rent increase is invalid can [request a rent increase review](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/challenging-rent-increases-or-high-rent).

## How often rent can increase

A rental provider can only increase rent at certain times. The permitted frequency depends on:

- the type of rental agreement
- when the agreement started
- whether the agreement allows rent increases.

In most cases, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months, even if the rental agreement specifies an earlier increase.

### Fixed-term agreements of five years or less

A rental agreement with an end date is a fixed-term agreement. Most fixed-term agreements are five years or less.

The rental provider must generally wait until the agreement ends before increasing the rent unless the agreement permits an increase during the fixed term.

If the agreement permits an increase:

- For agreements that started before 19 June 2019, rent cannot be increased more than once every six months.
- For agreements that started on or after 19 June 2019, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months.
- For rooming-house, residential-park or caravan-park agreements that started after 29 March 2021, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months.

### Fixed-term agreements longer than five years

A fixed-term rental agreement lasting more than five years is called a long-term rental agreement. These agreements were previously called long-term leases.

Under a long-term rental agreement, rent can only be increased when:

- the agreement permits rent increases
- at least 12 months have passed since the previous increase.

### Periodic agreements

A periodic rental agreement does not have an end date. It is often described as a month-by-month agreement.

A fixed-term agreement usually becomes periodic automatically when it ends unless the parties enter into another fixed-term agreement.

For periodic agreements:

- If the agreement started before 19 June 2019, rent cannot be increased more than once every six months.
- If the agreement became periodic on or after 19 June 2019, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months.

These limits continue to apply even if the agreement has been periodic for several years.

After receiving a rent increase, the renter can:

- accept the increase
- [request a rent assessment](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/challenging-rent-increases-or-high-rent)
- end the agreement by giving a [notice of intention to vacate](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/giving-notice-as-a-renter/renter-giving-notice).

### Social housing

Special rent rules apply to social housing.

Social-housing rent may be based on:

- the market rental rate
- the renter’s income.

The market rate is assessed once a year, while income is assessed twice a year. A change in either assessment may change the amount of rent payable.

See [market rent and fixed rent in social housing](https://www.housing.vic.gov.au/what-you-pay) for more information.

## Telling renters about rent increases

A rental provider must notify the renter at least 90 days before a rent increase takes effect. They must use the appropriate Notice of proposed rent increase form.

The notice must explain:

- the amount of the rent increase
- how the increase was calculated
- what the renter can do if they believe the increase is too high.

If rent is increased during a fixed-term agreement, the agreement must state the method used to calculate the increase. Examples include:

- Consumer Price Index
- rent index
- fixed percentage
- fixed dollar amount.

### Required notice forms

A rent increase is not valid unless the rental provider uses the correct form.

The applicable forms are:

- [Notice of proposed rent increase to renter of rented premises (Word, 85 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-proposed-rent-increase-to-renter-of-rented-premises.docx)
- [Notice of proposed rent increase to resident of rooming house (Word, 76 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-proposed-rent-increase-to-resident-of-a-rooming-house.docx)
- [Notice of proposed rent or hiring charge increase to residents of caravan park (Word, 82 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-rent-hiring-charge-increase-to-residents-of-caravan-park.docx)
- [Notice of site rent increase—fixed amount under site agreement (Word, 70 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-site-rent-increase-fixed-amount.docx)
- [Notice of proposed site rent increase—non-fixed amount (Word, 82 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-of-proposed-site-rent-increase-nonfixed-amount.docx)

If the rental provider does not use the correct form, the notice is invalid. The renter only has to pay the increased amount after receiving valid notice.

## Rent cannot be increased to penalise a renter

A rental provider cannot increase rent as a penalty because the renter has breached a term of the rental agreement.

A rental agreement must not contain a term requiring the renter to pay higher rent after breaching the agreement. A prohibited term:

- cannot be enforced
- may result in financial penalties for the rental provider.

A renter who finds such a term in their agreement should [contact Consumer Affairs Victoria](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/contact-us).

See [penalties under Victorian rental laws](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/legal-and-dispute-support/penalties) for further information.

## Changes to the bond when rent increases

An additional bond can only be requested when a long-term rental agreement is renewed for another fixed term of more than five years.

The rental provider must:

- give the renter 120 days’ written notice
- use the [Notice requesting additional bond](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-requesting-additional-bond.docx)
- request an additional bond no more than once every five years
- lodge the additional bond with the RTBA within 10 business days after receiving it.

The renter must pay the additional bond within the 120-day notice period.

An additional bond cannot be required for:

- a periodic rental agreement
- a short-term fixed rental agreement.

### Calculating the additional bond

To calculate the additional bond:

1. Determine the total bond that would normally be payable for the next five years of the agreement.
2. Subtract the bond already held by the [Residential Tenancies Bond Authority](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/about-us/statutory-bodies-and-committees/residential-tenancies-bond-authority).

The renter can [apply to VCAT](https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/case-types/residential-tenancies/apply-residential-tenancies) to request:

- a change to the rental agreement preventing the rental provider from requiring an additional bond
- a change to the amount of additional bond payable.

The correct form must be selected for the relevant accommodation type:

## Sections of the Act

Rent increases are covered by the following sections of the [Residential Tenancies Act 1997](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/residential-tenancies-act-1997):

- **Section 44:** Rent increases under residential rental agreements.
- **Section 101:** Required notice of a rent increase for a rooming house.
- **Section 152:** Required notice of a rent or hiring-charge increase for a caravan park.
- **Section 206SA:** Rent increases under Part 4A site agreements.
