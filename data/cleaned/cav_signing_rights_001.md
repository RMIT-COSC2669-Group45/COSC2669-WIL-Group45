# Knowing your rights when signing a rental agreement

A rental agreement is a contract between a renter and a rental provider. There is no cooling-off period after the renter signs the agreement. Renters should therefore understand the agreement before signing it.

## Making sure the property meets minimum standards

A rental provider must ensure that the property meets Victoria’s [15 minimum rental standards](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/minimum-standards-for-rental-properties):

- before advertising the property
- before the renter moves into the property.

Renters should inspect the property before signing a rental agreement.

If the property does not meet the minimum standards:

- the renter can end the rental agreement before moving in
- after moving in, the renter can request an urgent repair to bring the property up to the minimum standards.

## Using the correct rental agreement

A prescribed form must be used when entering into a written rental agreement. A prescribed form is a form required by Victorian rental law.

Consumer Affairs Victoria recommends using its official rental agreement form. See [residential rental agreements and official forms](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/residential-rental-agreements) before signing an agreement.

A renter or rental provider can request that additional conditions be included in the agreement. For example, an agreement may contain a condition prohibiting smoking inside the property.

However, an additional condition is invalid if it is inconsistent with:

- the Residential Tenancies Act 1997
- the standard rental agreement.

For example, a rental provider cannot include a condition allowing them to inspect the property every month.

See the list of [conditions prohibited in residential rental agreements](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/residential-rental-agreements).

## Receiving notices and documents electronically

A renter and rental provider can agree to exchange notices and other documents electronically, usually by email. This agreement should be recorded in the rental agreement.

When agreeing to electronic communication:

- each party should provide an email address that they check regularly
- a renter can withdraw their consent at any time by notifying the rental provider
- withdrawing consent should preferably be done in writing
- either party must notify the other immediately if their email address changes.

## Paying rent and fees

A renter must be offered at least one rent-payment method that does not require them to pay a fee. This could include:

- direct bank transfer
- direct debit.

A rental provider cannot ask for or accept more than one month’s rent in advance unless the weekly rent is more than $900.

See [rent payments and rent in advance](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/rent-payments-and-rent-in-advance) for further information.

A rental provider cannot charge a renter for:

- preparing the rental agreement
- providing one complete set of property keys to each renter named in the agreement.

## Bond amounts

In most cases, a rental provider cannot request a bond greater than one month’s rent.

See [bond amounts and payments](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/bond/bond-amounts-and-paying-a-bond) for exceptions and additional requirements.

## Before signing a rental agreement

Entering into an agreement to rent a property creates a residential rental agreement. It is a legal agreement that can take time to end, so renters should make sure the property is suitable before signing.

Before the agreement is signed, the rental provider must disclose:

- whether they intend to sell the property
- whether they have engaged an agent to sell the property or entered into a contract of sale
- whether a mortgagee is taking action to possess the property or has commenced proceedings to enforce a mortgage
- whether they own the property or otherwise have the legal right to rent it out
- whether electricity is supplied through an embedded electricity network
- whether, to the best of their knowledge, the property or common property was the location of a homicide within the previous five years
- whether the property meets the rental minimum standards
- whether they received a repair notice concerning structural mould or damp within the previous three years
- the date of the most recent electricity safety check
- the date of the most recent gas safety check
- whether any recommendations from electricity or gas safety checks remain outstanding
- whether the property is heritage listed
- whether, to the best of their knowledge, the property was used for trafficking or cultivating a drug of dependence within the previous five years
- whether, to the best of their knowledge, the property contains asbestos
- whether the property is affected by a building or planning application lodged with a relevant planning authority
- whether the property or common property is subject to an official notice, order, declaration, report or recommendation concerning building defects or safety
- whether there is a current domestic building-work dispute concerning the property under the *Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995*
- whether there is a current dispute affecting the property under Part 10 of the *Owners Corporations Act 2006*
- whether the property is subject to owners-corporation rules.

### Embedded electricity network information

If the property receives electricity through an embedded network, the rental provider must disclose:

- the embedded network’s trading name
- the Australian Business Number of the network operator
- the operator’s contact details
- applicable electricity tariffs
- other applicable fees
- where the renter can access this information, if it is not supplied directly.

### Building and safety notices

If an official notice, order, declaration, report or recommendation applies to the property, the rental provider must provide a description of it.

If owners-corporation rules apply, the rental provider must attach those rules to the rental application form.

## Terms that cannot be included in a rental agreement

A renter or rental provider can request additional terms and conditions. However, prohibited terms are invalid and cannot be enforced.

A rental provider who includes a prohibited term may face penalties.

See [conditions that cannot be included in a residential rental agreement](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/residential-rental-agreements#conditions-that-cannot-be-in-an-agreement).

## What rental providers must give renters

At the start of every rental agreement, the rental provider or agent must give the renter:

- a copy of the written rental agreement
- a paper copy of the [Renters guide](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/resources-and-guides-for-renters/renters-guide), or a digital copy if the renter agreed to receive documents electronically
- a telephone number for arranging urgent repairs outside business hours
- the rental provider’s or agent’s full name
- a postal address for sending notices and documents
- an email address if electronic delivery of notices and documents was agreed to in writing
- one set of keys for every renter who signed the agreement
- two paper copies of the condition report, or one electronic copy.

If a bond is payable, the rental provider must also give the renter a bond-lodgement form so the bond can be lodged with the [Residential Tenancies Bond Authority](https://www.rentalbonds.vic.gov.au/).

If an owners corporation applies to the property, the renter must also receive a copy of its rules.

## Checklist for signing a rental agreement

Before signing, the renter should confirm that:

- the agreement is complete and does not contain blank sections
- the bond is no more than one month’s rent, unless the weekly rent is more than $900
- they are not required to pay more than one month’s rent in advance, unless the weekly rent is more than $900 and they choose to do so
- at least one fee-free method of paying rent is available, excluding fees charged by the renter’s own bank
- any requested additional conditions have been discussed and recorded in the agreement
- they have not been charged for preparing the rental agreement.

An additional condition could, for example, require the rental provider to replace an oven within six months.

The renter should also review and agree to any negotiable terms concerning:

- pets
- cleaning
- gardening, mowing or general maintenance
- alterations or other changes to the property.
