# Notice to vacate in rental properties

Related information is available about:

- [Renters giving notice](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/giving-notice-as-a-renter/renter-giving-notice)
- [Evictions and possession orders](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/evictions-and-possession-orders)

Different notice rules apply to:

- [Rooming-house residents](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/notice-to-vacate/giving-notice-to-a-rooming-house-resident)
- [Caravan-park residents](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/notice-to-vacate/giving-notice-to-a-caravan-park-resident)
- [Site tenants in residential parks or villages](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/notice-to-vacate/giving-notice-to-a-site-tenant-in-a-park)

## What is a notice to vacate?

When a rental provider wants a renter to move out, the rental provider can:

- discuss ending the agreement with the renter and reach an agreement
- give the renter a formal notice to vacate.

A notice to vacate is a formal statement that the rental provider wants to end the rental agreement. A rental provider can only issue one for a legally valid reason.

If the renter does not leave by the termination date stated in the notice, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for an [eviction or possession order](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/evictions-and-possession-orders).

## Reasons rental providers can and cannot give notice

A rental provider must:

- give a legally valid reason for issuing a notice to vacate
- state that reason in the notice
- comply with the applicable notice period and other requirements.

A notice issued without a valid reason is not valid.

In some circumstances, a rental provider can issue a notice before the rental agreement’s stated end date.

A rental provider cannot issue a notice because a renter exercised, or said they would exercise, a legal right. For example, a notice cannot be issued because the renter:

- requested repairs
- asked for permission to keep a pet
- challenged a rent increase.

A notice to vacate is also invalid if it involves [unlawful discrimination](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/legal-and-dispute-support/unlawful-discrimination).

### Agreement to leave without formal notice

A rental provider may ask a renter to leave without issuing a formal notice to vacate.

The renter:

- does not have to agree to leave
- should record the agreement in writing if they agree.

### Immediate notice

A rental provider can issue an immediate notice to vacate if the renter or the renter’s visitor:

- intentionally or recklessly causes serious damage to the property, safety equipment or common areas
- puts neighbours, the rental provider, the rental provider’s agent, contractors or employees in danger.

See [immediate notices](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/immediate-notice).

Additional rules apply to:

- [Violent or dangerous behaviour in rooming houses](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/violent-or-dangerous-behaviour-rooming-houses)
- [Violent or dangerous behaviour in caravan parks](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/violent-or-dangerous-behaviour-caravan-parks)

## How to give a notice to vacate

A rental provider should use the [Notice to vacate to renter or renters of rented premises (Word, 760 KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-to-vacate-to-renter-of-rented-premises.docx).

The notice must:

- be addressed to the renter
- state the reason for ending the rental agreement
- be signed by the rental provider or their agent
- allow enough time for delivery
- state the date by which the renter must leave, known as the termination date.

### Delivering a notice to vacate

The notice must be delivered to the renter at the rented premises using one of the following methods:

- registered post
- electronic communication, such as email, if the renter consented to receive notices electronically
- personal delivery by hand.

When sending the notice by post, the rental provider must:

- use registered post
- consider the delivery method and posting location
- allow sufficient time for delivery.

See [Australia Post delivery estimates](https://auspost.com.au/sending/delivery-speeds-and-coverage).

## Challenging a notice to vacate

A renter can challenge a notice to vacate if they:

- believe it was not delivered correctly
- disagree with the reason stated in the notice
- received the notice because of family violence.

See [challenging a notice to vacate](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/notice-to-vacate/challenging-a-notice-to-vacate).

## When a renter does not leave

If a renter does not leave after receiving a valid notice to vacate, the rental provider can apply for a possession order.

See [possession orders and evictions](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/evictions-and-immediate-notice/evictions-and-possession-orders).

## When a renter dies

If a property has only one renter and that renter dies, the rental provider can end the agreement by giving a notice to vacate to:

- the deceased renter’s legal personal representative
- the deceased renter’s next of kin.

See [what happens when a renter or rental provider dies](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/moving-out-giving-notice-and-evictions/if-a-renter-or-rental-provider-dies).

## Ending an agreement early—reasons and notice periods

A rental provider can only end a rental agreement before its stated end date for a legally recognised reason.

A notice to vacate is invalid if the rental provider does not give one of the permitted reasons.

The minimum notice period depends on the reason for ending the agreement.

Related information includes:

- [Late or unpaid rent](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/rent/late-or-unpaid-rent)
- [Keeping pets without consent](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/pets)
- [Not paying an agreed bond](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/bond/bond-amounts-and-paying-a-bond)

### Reasons for ending an agreement early

| Reason for notice to vacate | Minimum notice required |
|---|---:|
| The renter or their visitor intentionally or recklessly causes serious damage to the property, including safety equipment or common areas. | Immediate |
| The renter or their visitor puts neighbours, the rental provider, the rental provider’s agent, contractors or employees in danger. | Immediate |
| The premises are unfit for human habitation, completely destroyed or damaged to the extent that they are unsafe. | Immediate |
| The renter or another person living at the property seriously threatens or intimidates the rental provider, their agent, contractors or employees. | 14 days |
| The renter owes at least 14 days’ rent. | 14 days |
| The renter has failed to comply with a VCAT compliance order. | 14 days |
| The renter has received two breach-of-duty notices and commits the same breach again. | 14 days |
| The property is being used for illegal purposes. | 14 days |
| The renter has brought in other renters or subletters without consent. | 14 days |
| The renter has not paid the bond as agreed. | 14 days |
| The renter has a child under 16 living at the premises when the rental agreement states this is not allowed. | 14 days |
| The rental provider is a government housing authority and the renter misled the authority to obtain social housing. | 14 days |
| The renter has been involved in drug-related activity in public housing. | 14 days |
| The renter is keeping a pet without consent and VCAT has made an order excluding the pet. | 28 days |

## Notice to vacate at the end of an agreement

A rental provider cannot issue a notice to vacate at the end of a fixed-term rental agreement without a valid reason.

Generally, the minimum notice period for a valid reason is 90 days. A shorter period applies in certain circumstances.

Some reasons require supporting evidence, such as:

- a building permit
- a statutory declaration
- a contract or quotation
- evidence that a property is being sold.

If the required evidence is not included, the notice may be invalid.

### Reasons for giving notice at the end of an agreement

For a fixed-term rental agreement:

- the termination date must be on or after the agreement’s end date
- the notice must state a valid reason.

For a periodic, or month-to-month, agreement, a rental provider can give notice at any time, but must provide a valid reason.

Some reasons require documentary evidence. If the required evidence is not provided with the notice, the notice is invalid.

#### Rental provider plans to move into the property

**Minimum notice:** 14 days.

This reason must have been included in the additional terms of the fixed-term rental agreement.

The notice must include both:

- the rental agreement showing the relevant additional term, as required by section 91ZW
- a witnessed [statutory declaration](https://www.justice.vic.gov.au/statdecs) from the rental provider confirming the date they intend to occupy the property.

If there is more than one rental provider, each provider must supply a statutory declaration.

#### Reconstruction, repairs or renovations

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

This reason applies when the planned work cannot be completed while the renter remains in the property and all necessary permits have been obtained.

For repairs, the notice must include both:

- photographic evidence showing that repairs are required
- a contract or quotation from a suitably qualified tradesperson.

The contract or quotation must state:

1. the nature of the required repairs
2. why the renter must vacate for the repairs to be completed
3. the estimated time required to complete the repairs.

Alternatively, the rental provider can provide a building permit for repairs or renovations.

A building permit is the only documentary evidence accepted to support a notice issued specifically for renovations.

#### Property will be demolished

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

The notice must include both:

- a building permit for demolition
- a contract with a suitably qualified builder-demolisher stating when demolition will occur.

Unless VCAT gives permission, the rental provider must not re-let the property as a residence within six months after issuing the notice.

#### Property will be used for another purpose

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

This includes situations where the rental provider intends to use the property for a business instead of as a residence.

The notice must include a witnessed statutory declaration that:

- identifies the proposed business use
- provides details of the business
- states that the property will not be re-let as a residence within six months after the notice was issued.

If there is more than one rental provider, each provider must supply a statutory declaration.

The rental provider must also provide at least one of the following:

- the business’s Australian Business Number
- business registration or licence
- a council planning permit.

#### Rental provider, family member or dependent will move in

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

This reason can apply when the intended occupant is:

- the rental provider
- a member of the rental provider’s immediate family, including a parent or parent-in-law
- a dependent who normally lives with the rental provider.

The notice must include a witnessed statutory declaration stating either:

- that the rental provider intends to occupy the property
- or the intended occupant’s name, their relationship to the rental provider and whether they are a dependent.

The declaration must also confirm that the rental provider understands that the property cannot be re-let as a residence to someone other than the named occupant within six months after the notice was issued, unless VCAT permits it.

If there is more than one rental provider, each provider must supply a statutory declaration.

#### Property will be sold or offered for sale

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

The property must be vacated immediately after the rental agreement ends. The rental provider cannot shorten the agreement to issue a notice for this reason.

The notice must include one of the following:

- a dated contract of sale signed by the vendor and purchaser
- a contract of engagement or authority to sell with a licensed estate agent
- a contract of sale prepared by a conveyancer or Australian legal practitioner.

Unless VCAT gives permission, the property must not be re-let as a residence within six months after the notice was issued.

#### Government authority requires the property for public purposes

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

The notice must include one of the following:

- publicly available acquisition details
- a compulsory acquisition letter from the government.

#### Renter no longer qualifies for social housing

**Minimum notice:** 90 days.

This reason applies when:

- the rental provider is a government housing authority
- the renter no longer meets the eligibility criteria for social housing.

No specific documentary evidence is listed on the source page.

## Mortgagee giving notice to vacate

A mortgagee, such as a bank, can give the renter a notice to vacate when repossessing the property.

Specific legal requirements apply to notices issued by mortgagees. Refer to the *Residential Tenancies Act 1997* or [contact Consumer Affairs Victoria](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/contact-us) for further information.
