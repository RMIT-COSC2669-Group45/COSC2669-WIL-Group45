# Condition reports

## What is a condition report?
A condition report is a record of a property’s condition at the start of a rental agreement. Rental agreements include those for:

- rooming houses
- caravan parks
- caravans
- Part 4A sites

Anything that is unclean, damaged or not working should be included in the condition report. The report should cover the property, room or caravan, including:

- its fittings and fixtures
- everything inside and outside

All rental properties, rooming houses, caravans, caravan sites and Part 4A park sites must have condition reports.

> **Terminology note:**
>
> - ‘Rental provider’ includes landlords, rooming house operators, caravan park owners, caravan owners and Part 4A site owners.
> - ‘Renter’ includes renters, rooming house residents, park residents and site tenants.

The rental provider, or their agent, prepares the condition report. They must sign it and give two copies to the renter, or one copy if sending it electronically, before the renter moves in.

### Why is the condition report important?
Condition reports can be important evidence if there is a dispute about [bond claims](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/bond/bond-claims-and-refunds) or who should pay for cleaning or repairing damage.

## Condition reports at the start of the agreement
The rental provider is responsible for providing two copies of the signed condition report, or one electronic copy, to the renter before they move in. The report must be created using the [Condition report template (Word, 187KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/condition-report-word.docx). It can be provided on paper or electronically, such as by email.

The renter should check the report. If anything is inaccurate, they can change it and add their own comments. The renter should send a signed copy to the rental provider or agent and keep one for themselves.

The renter must return the report within five business days of moving into the property. Business days do not include weekends or public holidays. The renter should allow enough time for delivery if sending it by post.

If a renter is not given a condition report before moving in, they can complete their own. They should give it to the rental provider or agent within five business days of moving in.

Both the renter and rental provider should take photographs of the property at the start of the rental agreement, particularly if there is pre-existing damage.

Photographs should be dated, labelled and attached to the condition report. They can be kept as hard copies or digital files.

A condition report that records something requiring repair gives the rental provider written notice of the issue. If the repairs are not completed within a reasonable period, the renter can apply to VCAT for an order requiring the repairs to be completed.

The rental provider or agent and the renter must keep a copy of the condition report until the rental agreement ends.

### Report an issue with an advertised rental property
Prospective renters, real estate agents or members of the public who have concerns about an advertised rental property can [report an issue with an advertised rental property](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/applying-signing-and-moving-in/applying-for-a-property/report-an-issue-with-an-advertised-rental-property) to Consumer Affairs Victoria.

### Disputes about the condition report
It is best for the renter and rental provider to work together to resolve disagreements about the condition report.

If the renter or rental provider believes the condition report is incorrect or incomplete, either party can [apply to VCAT](https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/case-types/residential-tenancies/apply-residential-tenancies) for a decision. They must apply within 30 days of the start of the rental agreement.

## Condition reports at the end of the agreement
When a rental agreement ends, the rental provider or agent must complete the ‘Exit condition report’ section of the original condition report within 10 days.

The renter must be present when this happens or must have been given a reasonable opportunity to be present. Reasonable means what most people would consider fair.

The exit condition report records the condition of the property at the end of the tenancy.

## Claiming the bond using the condition report
A rental provider or agent may be able to use the condition report to claim part or all of the bond at the end of a rental agreement if:

- there is new damage to the property, room, hired caravan or site that is not normal wear and tear
- the property needs new repairs
- the property requires further cleaning
- items are missing

If there is a dispute about the bond, either the renter or rental provider can [apply to VCAT](https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/case-types/residential-tenancies/apply-residential-tenancies). This process is free if the claim is for an amount less than the bond paid.

Read more about [claiming the bond](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/bond/bond-claims-and-refunds).

## Condition of the property and repairs
There are minimum standards for the condition of [rental properties](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/minimum-standards-for-rental-properties) and [rooming houses](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/rooming-house-minimum-standards).

There are also rules concerning:

- [repairs and urgent repairs in rental properties and rooming houses](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/repairs/repairs-in-rental-properties)
- responsibility for managing [pests and mould](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/pests-infestations-and-mould)
- renters [installing fixtures and making changes to the property](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/renters-making-changes-to-the-property)

## Cleaning responsibilities
Renters and residents must keep the property in a reasonably clean condition.

When moving out, renters and residents must leave the property:

- reasonably clean
- in the same condition as when they moved in, taking into account fair wear and tear

For rental agreements signed from 29 March 2021, additional requirements apply to professional cleaning. Rental providers cannot require renters to arrange professional cleaning unless the property:

- needs to be returned to the condition it was in at the start of the rental agreement, allowing for fair wear and tear; for example, if it was professionally cleaned before the renter moved in
- is in a condition that requires professional cleaning

Read more in [Guideline 2 – Cleanliness (Word, 244KB)](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/library/publications/housing-and-accommodation/renting/guideline-2--cleanliness.docx).
