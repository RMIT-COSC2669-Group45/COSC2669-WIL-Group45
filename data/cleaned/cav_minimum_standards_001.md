# Rental properties—minimum standards

From 25 November 2025, rental properties must meet the minimum standards before they are advertised.

From 1 December 2025, cords on internal window coverings must be secured to prevent them from forming loops.

## Rental properties must meet the minimum standards

There are 15 categories of minimum standards for rental properties. Every rental property must comply with the requirements in each applicable category.

Rental providers and agents must ensure that a property meets the minimum standards:

- when the property is advertised or offered for rent
- before the renter moves into the property.

The detailed requirements are provided in the [15 categories of minimum standards](#minimum-standard-categories) section of this document.

Different requirements apply to [minimum standards in rooming houses](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/rooming-house-minimum-standards).

## When minimum standards apply

A rental property must meet the minimum standards before it is advertised.

For existing rental agreements, the minimum standards in Schedule 4 of the [Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/statutory-rules/residential-tenancies-regulations-2021/002) apply when the agreement:

- started on or after 29 March 2021
- started before 29 March 2021 but became a periodic, or month-to-month, agreement on or after 29 March 2021.

## When minimum standards do not apply

The minimum standards do not apply to an agreement that became a periodic, or month-to-month, agreement before 29 March 2021.

## Renters’ rights when minimum standards are not met

Renters have rights when a property does not comply with the minimum standards, including when the rental provider fails to complete the required repairs.

See [renters’ rights when minimum standards are not met](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/minimum-standards/renters-rights-when-minimum-standards-are-not-met).

## Minimum-standard categories

New minimum rental standards for energy efficiency will take effect from 1 March 2027. See [new minimum energy-efficiency standards](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/legislation/public-consultations-and-reviews/new-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards).

### Bathrooms

A rental property’s bathroom must have:

- a washbasin
- a shower or bath
- a reasonable supply of hot and cold water.

The shower must have a shower head with a three-star water-efficiency rating.

If a three-star shower head cannot be installed, for example because of the property’s age, a one-star or two-star shower head is acceptable.

### Electrical safety

The electrical-safety minimum standard commenced on 29 March 2023.

Rental properties must have a modern switchboard containing:

- circuit breakers
- electrical safety switches.

Electrical safety switches include residual current devices known as RCDs, RCCBs or RCBOs.

The rental provider must engage an electrician to ensure the property complies with the electrical-safety standard.

#### Properties with a circuit-breaker switchboard

An electrician may add circuit-breaker components to the power-outlet and lighting circuits without modifying the protection for other circuits.

The required components may include:

- an overcurrent circuit breaker and residual current device
- an overcurrent circuit breaker and residual current-operated circuit breaker without integral overcurrent protection
- a residual current-operated circuit breaker with integral overcurrent protection.

#### Properties without a circuit-breaker switchboard

Some older properties have panel-and-fuse boards or federal fuse boards instead of circuit-breaker switchboards.

If circuit-breaker components cannot be added to the existing board, an electrician may need to replace the entire switchboard.

In this situation, circuit-breaker protection must be provided for all circuits, including:

- power outlets and lighting
- fixed cooking equipment
- hot-water units
- air-conditioning circuits.

### Heating

Every rental property must have a fixed heater in good working order in the main living area. A portable heater does not satisfy this requirement.

For rental agreements entered into from 29 March 2023, the main living area must have an energy-efficient fixed heater. If an existing fixed heater is not energy efficient, the rental provider must upgrade it.

An energy-efficient fixed heater must be one of the following:

- a non-ducted air conditioner or heat pump with an energy rating of at least two stars
- a gas space heater with an energy rating of at least two stars
- a ducted or hydronic heating system with an outlet in the main living area
- a domestic solid-fuel appliance, such as a fireplace or wood-burning stove.

#### Heating requirements for apartments

Installing an energy-efficient heater may be considered unreasonable in some apartment buildings if:

- the installation cost would be significantly higher than the average installation cost in a domestic apartment building
- owners-corporation rules prohibit the installation
- another Act or local law makes the installation cost prohibitive.

Even when installing an energy-efficient heater is unreasonable, the rental provider must still install a working fixed heater in the main living area.

The rental provider should retain evidence explaining why installation of an energy-efficient heater is unreasonable and tell the renter before entering into the rental agreement.

### Kitchen

The property must have a kitchen containing:

- a dedicated cooking and food-preparation area
- a working sink connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water
- a working stovetop with at least two burners.

If the property has an oven, it must be in good working order.

These requirements do not apply when the property:

- is listed on the [Heritage Council Victoria register](https://heritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/heritage-protection/register/)
- has an approved exemption from the standard.

### Laundry

If the property has a laundry, it must be connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water.

### Lighting

Rooms, corridors and hallways must have enough light to make them functional.

During the day, natural light may be received from an adjoining room. At night, renters must have access to artificial light.

These requirements do not apply if the property is registered under the *Heritage Act 2017* and has an approved exemption.

### Locks

External entry doors must have:

- functioning deadlocks
- or locks that require a key to unlock from outside but can be unlocked without a key from inside.

A deadlock is a deadlatch containing at least one cylinder.

A deadlock is not required when:

- the door cannot be secured with a deadlock because of its position
- it is a screen door in the same frame as an external door
- another Act or law requires a different lock or security device
- another security barrier prevents access to the door, such as a locked apartment-building entrance or gate
- the property is registered under the *Heritage Act 2017* and has an approved exemption.

See [locks and security](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/keeping-the-property-safe/locks-and-security).

### Mould and damp

All rooms must be free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure.

### Structural soundness

The property must be:

- structurally sound
- weatherproof.

### Toilets

The toilet must be in good working order and connected to:

- a reticulated sewerage system
- a wastewater-treatment system permitted under EPA Victoria’s onsite wastewater-management requirements
- another system approved by the local council.

The toilet must be located in a separate room, either:

- by itself
- in a bathroom
- in a combined bathroom and laundry.

See [EPA Victoria](https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/) and [Victorian local councils](https://www.vic.gov.au/know-your-council) for relevant requirements.

### Ventilation

The property must have adequate ventilation in all habitable rooms, including:

- bathrooms
- showers
- toilets
- laundries.

The property must satisfy the applicable ventilation requirements of the Building Code of Australia.

See the [Australian Building Codes Board resource library](https://www.abcb.gov.au/resources).

### Vermin-proof bins

The rental provider must supply:

- a rubbish bin
- a recycling bin.

The bins may be supplied by the local council or purchased elsewhere. They must:

- prevent access by vermin such as rats and mice
- meet local-council collection requirements.

### Windows

Every external window capable of opening must have a functioning latch that secures it against external entry.

An openable window must also be capable of remaining in either an open or closed position.

### Window coverings

Windows in rooms likely to be used as bedrooms or living areas must have curtains or blinds that:

- can be closed
- block light
- provide privacy.

### Window-covering anchors

From 1 December 2025, every corded internal window covering must have an anchor that secures its cords and prevents them from forming loops.

This requirement applies to corded blinds, curtains and similar internal window coverings.

Renters and rental providers can [order a free curtain and blind cord-safety kit](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/consumers-and-businesses/products-and-services/product-safety/curtain-and-blind-cord-safety) or purchase one from a hardware store.

## Regulations

Rental-property minimum standards are specified in [Schedule 4 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/statutory-rules/residential-tenancies-regulations-2021).

Requirements for window-covering anchors are addressed in the [Residential Tenancies Amendment (Housing Statement Reform and Part 4A Site Agreements) Regulations](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/as-made/statutory-rules/residential-tenancies-amendment-housing-statement-reform-and-part-4a-site-agreements-regulations).

## Sections of the Act

Rental-property minimum standards are addressed by the following sections of the [Residential Tenancies Act 1997](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/residential-tenancies-act-1997/104):

- **Section 65A:** Occupation of rented premises that do not comply with rental minimum standards.
- **Section 65B:** Advertising premises that do not comply with rental minimum standards.
- **Section 72:** Urgent repairs.
- **Section 77:** Payment of rent into the Rent Special Account.
